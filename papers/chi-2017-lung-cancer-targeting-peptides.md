## Summary

This study identified three novel lung cancer-targeting peptides (HSP1, HSP2, HSP4) via phage display biopanning that bind to multiple lung cancer subtypes including small cell lung cancer (SCLC), adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and large cell carcinoma. The peptides were applied for multimodal molecular imaging and targeted liposomal drug delivery, demonstrating significant therapeutic improvement in orthotopic lung cancer mouse models.

## Background & Motivation

- Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide
- Current targeted therapies (e.g., TKIs like erlotinib/gefitinib) are effective only for specific subtypes with particular mutations (e.g., EGFR-mutant NSCLC)
- No broadly effective targeting agents existed for large cell carcinoma and SCLC
- Need for targeting ligands that work across multiple lung cancer histotypes for both imaging and therapy

## Methods

- **Phage display biopanning**: Screened a phage-displayed peptide library against lung cancer cell lines to identify cancer-binding peptides
- **Peptide synthesis**: Produced synthetic peptides (HSP1, HSP2, HSP4) from the identified phage clones
- **Binding validation**: Flow cytometry, immunofluorescence, and tissue microarray staining on 120+ clinical specimens
- **Imaging**: SPION (superparamagnetic iron oxide nanoparticle) conjugation for MRI; near-infrared fluorescence imaging
- **Drug delivery**: Conjugation of peptides to liposomal doxorubicin (PLD)
- **In vivo models**: Subcutaneous and orthotopic xenograft mouse models

## Key Peptides Identified

| Peptide | Sequence | Primary Utility | Key Property |
|---------|----------|-----------------|--------------|
| HSP1 | GAMHLPWHMGTL | Molecular imaging | Strong surface binding; minimal internalization |
| HSP2 | NPWEEQGYRYSM | Drug delivery | Moderate endocytosis |
| HSP4 | NNPWREMMYIEI | Intracellular drug delivery | Preferentially endocytosed; receptor-mediated uptake |

**Consensus motifs** identified across phage clones: MHL-W, NPW-E, W-EMM

## Mechanism of Action

1. **Surface binding**: HSP1 binds tightly to cancer cell surfaces, making it suitable for imaging applications where prolonged surface retention is desirable
2. **Receptor-mediated endocytosis**: HSP4 is preferentially internalized into cancer cells, enabling intracellular delivery of conjugated therapeutics
3. **Multi-subtype targeting**: All three peptides recognize targets expressed across SCLC, adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and large cell carcinoma, but not normal lung tissue
4. **Enhanced drug uptake**: Peptide-targeted liposomal doxorubicin showed 9-13 fold reduction in IC50 values compared to untargeted drug in vitro

## Key Results

### Imaging
- HSP1-conjugated SPIONs achieved ~42% reduction in T2-weighted MRI signal intensity in tumors
- Near-infrared fluorescence imaging confirmed tumor-specific accumulation

### Therapeutic Efficacy
- HSP2/HSP4-conjugated PLD showed up to 2-fold increased tumor accumulation vs untargeted PLD
- HSP4-PLD combination therapy in A549 orthotopic models:
  - Extended median survival by 47 days (131 vs 84 days, P=0.0248)
  - Significant tumor growth inhibition

### Tissue Microarray Validation
- Tested on 120+ clinical lung cancer tissue samples across all four major subtypes
- Peptides stained cancer tissues positively while showing no reactivity with normal tissues

## Significance

- First report of peptide ligands capable of targeting all major lung cancer subtypes simultaneously
- Dual-purpose application: molecular imaging (diagnosis) + targeted drug delivery (therapy) — a theranostic approach
- Provides a platform for personalized combinational therapy using different peptide-drug conjugates based on their distinct cellular uptake properties

## Limitations & Future Directions

- Specific molecular targets (receptors) for these peptides remain unidentified
- Further clinical translation studies needed
- Potential for combination with existing targeted therapies or immunotherapies
