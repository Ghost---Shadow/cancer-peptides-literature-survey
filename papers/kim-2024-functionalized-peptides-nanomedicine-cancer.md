## Summary

A review of peptide-functionalized nanomedicine covering three main strategies: (1) cancer cell-targeting ligands, (2) self-assembling nanostructure building blocks, and (3) stimuli-responsive system elements. Covers applications across 10 cancer types using various nanoparticle platforms (gold NPs, MOFs, mesoporous silica, liposomes, polymeric NPs). Lung cancer applications include CPP33-modified ZIF-90 for siRNA delivery to A549 cells.

## Three Peptide-Nanomedicine Strategies

### 1. Cancer Cell-Targeting Ligands
Peptides conjugated to nanoparticle surfaces for selective cancer cell recognition:

| Peptide | Target | Nanoparticle | Cancer |
|---------|--------|-------------|--------|
| WXEAAYQRFL | MCF-7 cells | MSNP | Breast |
| WQPDTAHHWATL | PSMA | Gold NPs | Prostate |
| **CPP33** (RLWMRWYSPRTRAYG) | **Lung cancer cells** | **ZIF-90 MOF** | **Lung (A549)** |
| CPP44 (KRTPTMRFRYTWNPMK) | Liver cancer | IRMOF-8 | Liver |
| RGD | αvβ3 integrin | Various | Cervical |
| KTLLPTP | Pancreatic cancer | Liposomes | Pancreatic |

### 2. Self-Assembling Peptide Nanostructures
Peptides that spontaneously form nanostructures (spheres, fibers, tubes) for drug encapsulation — driven by hydrophobic interactions, hydrogen bonding, and electrostatic forces.

### 3. Stimuli-Responsive Systems
Peptides that respond to tumor microenvironment triggers:
- **pH-responsive**: Conformational change at acidic tumor pH
- **Enzyme-responsive**: Cleavage by overexpressed tumor proteases (MMP, cathepsins)
- **Redox-responsive**: GSH-triggered disulfide bond cleavage
- **Light-responsive**: Photocleavable peptide linkers

## Lung Cancer Application — CPP33-ZIF-90 System

- **Nanoplatform**: ZIF-90 metal-organic framework modified with CPP33 peptide
- **Cargo**: Survivin siRNA + Oridonin (natural terpenoid)
- **Target**: A549 NSCLC cells
- **Mechanism**: CPP33 enables cellular uptake → siRNA silences survivin (anti-apoptotic) → Oridonin provides direct cytotoxicity
- **In vivo**: Reduced tumor growth in tumor-bearing mice
- **Selectivity**: Demonstrated preferential uptake in lung cancer cells

## Nanoparticle Platforms Covered

| Platform | Advantages | Peptide Role |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| Gold NPs | Biocompatible, easy surface chemistry | Targeting ligand |
| ZIF-8/ZIF-90 (MOFs) | High drug loading, pH-responsive | Targeting + gating |
| Mesoporous silica | Large pore volume, tunable size | Targeting ligand |
| Liposomes | Biocompatible, clinical track record | Surface decoration |
| Polymeric NPs | Biodegradable, versatile | Self-assembly + targeting |

## Significance

- Comprehensive overview of three distinct peptide-nanomedicine integration strategies
- Covers 10 cancer types with specific peptide-nanoparticle-drug combinations
- Highlights MOF-based platforms as emerging nanocarriers for peptide-guided delivery
- Provides design principles for multi-functional peptide-nanoparticle systems

## Limitations

- Limited depth on any single application — broad but shallow coverage
- Lung cancer examples are limited (CPP33-ZIF-90 is the main one)
- No clinical trial data — all preclinical
- Some nanoparticle platforms (MOFs) face translation challenges (biodegradation, scale-up)
