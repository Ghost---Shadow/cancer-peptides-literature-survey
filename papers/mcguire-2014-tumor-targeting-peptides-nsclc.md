## Summary

The first comprehensive phage display biopanning study dedicated to isolating high-affinity peptide ligands for a single cancer type — non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Three phage-displayed peptide libraries were screened against 8 well-characterized NSCLC cell lines representing all major subtypes. Eleven novel tumor-targeting peptides were identified, showing distinct binding profiles across a panel of 40 NSCLC lines while sparing normal bronchial epithelial cells. The peptides homed to tumors in vivo and bound patient tumor samples.

## Background & Motivation

- Targeted therapies and molecular imaging depend on availability of high-affinity, tumor-specific ligands
- Existing targeting ligands for lung cancer were limited in number and diversity
- Need for ligands that distinguish between NSCLC subtypes and correlate with oncogenic genotypes
- Goal: systematically generate a panel of NSCLC-targeting peptides via phage display

## Methods

- **Phage display biopanning**: Three separate phage-displayed peptide libraries screened on NSCLC cells
- **Cell line panel**: 8 NSCLC lines used for biopanning covering all major subtypes:
  - Large cell carcinoma: H1299, H460, H1155, H6612
  - Adenocarcinoma: H2009, A549
  - Squamous cell carcinoma: HCC15, HCC95
- **Validation**: Binding profiled across 40 NSCLC cell lines + normal bronchial epithelial controls
- **Multimerization**: Peptides synthesized as monomers and tetramers to enhance avidity
- **In vivo**: Tumor homing assessed in xenograft models
- **Clinical validation**: Binding to patient-derived tumor specimens

## Key Results

### Binding Affinity
- Monomer peptides showed measurable but moderate binding
- **Tetramerization dramatically enhanced affinity**: KD values of **0.0071–40 nM**
- This represents a practical strategy for improving peptide performance

### Binding Specificity
- All peptides bound NSCLC cells but **did not bind normal bronchial epithelial cell lines**
- Each peptide showed a **distinct binding profile** across the 40-line NSCLC panel
- Different peptides preferentially bound different NSCLC subtypes

### Genotype Correlation
- Binding patterns of specific peptides **correlated with oncogenic genotypes** (EGFR, KRAS mutations)
- Correlated with **activation of specific signaling pathways**, particularly EGFR signaling
- Suggests peptides could serve as **surrogate biomarkers** for tumor molecular phenotype

### In Vivo Tumor Homing
- Peptides accumulated selectively in tumors in xenograft mouse models
- Confirmed tumor specificity in living organisms

### Patient Tumor Binding
- Peptides bound to patient-derived NSCLC tumor samples
- Validates clinical translatability

## Significance

- **First comprehensive biopanning** effort for peptide ligand discovery against a single cancer type
- Created the largest reported panel of tumor-targeting peptides for NSCLC
- Demonstrated that peptide binding profiles can reflect underlying tumor molecular biology
- Tetramerization strategy achieves sub-nanomolar affinity — competitive with antibodies
- Provides a toolkit of peptides with diverse binding specificities for personalized targeting

## Limitations

- Specific molecular targets (receptors) of these peptides were not identified
- Long-term in vivo pharmacokinetics and therapeutic efficacy not assessed
- Published in 2014 — subsequent development/clinical progress of these peptides is unclear
