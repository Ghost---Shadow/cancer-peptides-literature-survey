## Summary

A comprehensive review of peptide-based cancer treatment strategies covering anti-cancer peptides (ACPs), tumor-homing peptides, cell-penetrating peptides, design strategies, routes of administration, and diagnostic imaging. The paper catalogs 56 pre-clinical peptides and 10+ clinical-stage candidates, noting that despite extensive research, FDA-approved peptide agents for solid cancers remain limited.

## Background & Motivation

- Peptide-based strategies offer specificity, personalized design, small size, and simple production
- Major cancer treatment challenges: multidrug resistance and lack of tumor-specific therapies with minimal side effects
- Peptides bridge the gap between small molecules and large biologics (antibodies)

## Classification of Peptide Agents

### Anti-Cancer Peptides (ACPs)
| Mechanism | Examples | Mode of Action |
|-----------|----------|----------------|
| Apoptosis induction | AtMP1, AtMP2, MSP-4, p28 | Target BCL-2 family, activate TNF pathways, p53-mediated death |
| Membrane disruption | HPRP-A1-TAT, melittin | Exploit PS exposure on cancer cells; amphipathic lysis |
| Angiogenesis inhibition | QR-KLU, KV11, KAI | Block VEGFR2 signaling, c-Src/ERK pathways |
| Immunomodulation | Peptide vaccines (E75) | Activate T cells/NK cells, enhance immune recognition |

### Tumor-Homing Peptides (THPs)
| Peptide | Sequence | Target |
|---------|----------|--------|
| RGD-4C | CDCRGDCFCG | αvβ3/αvβ5 integrins |
| c(RGDyK) | cyclic RGDyK | αvβ3 integrin |
| iRGD | CRGDRPGC | Integrins + neuropilin-1 |
| CREKA | CREKA | Fibrin-fibronectin in tumor stroma |
| Octreotide | Modified somatostatin | SSTR receptors |

### Cell-Penetrating Peptides (CPPs)
| Peptide | Sequence | Application |
|---------|----------|-------------|
| TAT | YGRKKRRQRRR | Drug/gene delivery across membranes |
| Penetratin | RQIKIWFQNRRMKWKK | Intracellular cargo delivery |
| p28 | Azurin aa 50-77 | Direct anticancer + CPP activity |

## Peptide Design Strategies

1. **Cyclization** — improves metabolic stability and receptor binding; examples: NKTP-3, KRpep-2d, LUNA18
2. **D-amino acid substitution** — resists proteolytic degradation
3. **Polymer conjugation** — PEGylation extends half-life
4. **Nanoparticle assembly** — peptide-functionalized NPs for enhanced delivery
5. **Stapled peptides** — hydrocarbon cross-links stabilize alpha-helical structure (e.g., ALRN-6924)

## Lung Cancer-Relevant Findings

| Peptide | Target | Lung Cancer Application |
|---------|--------|------------------------|
| **NKTP-3** | NRP1 + KRASG12D | Cyclic D-peptide; inhibited A427 lung cancer cells; potent antitumor activity in xenograft and primary lung cancer models |
| **KRpep-2d** | K-RasG12D | Cyclic peptide from phage display; targets KRAS-mutant lung cancers |
| **LUNA18** | KRAS | Cyclic peptide by Chugai Pharmaceutical; Phase I/II clinical trials for solid tumors |
| **99mTc-3PRGD2** | αvβ3 integrin | Multicenter imaging studies show sensitivity for detecting lung malignancies |

## Key Conclusions

- Peptide-based agents demonstrate clear advantages in specificity and reduced toxicity
- Diagnostic/imaging peptides have achieved more clinical success than therapeutic peptides so far
- PRRT (Lutathera) represents the most successful peptide-based cancer therapy to date
- AI/ML approaches are highlighted as the future for accelerating peptide drug discovery
- Major remaining challenges: low oral bioavailability, short half-life, BBB penetration

## Significance

- Most comprehensive catalog of cancer peptide agents in recent literature (56 pre-clinical + 10+ clinical)
- Provides a roadmap of design strategies to overcome intrinsic peptide limitations
- Highlights the gap between pre-clinical promise and clinical approval for solid tumors

## Limitations

- Broad cancer focus — lung cancer coverage is limited to a few specific examples
- Clinical trial outcomes for many listed peptides remain pending
- Review scope ends June 2023
