## Summary

A comprehensive review synthesizing advances in nanotechnology-enabled lung cancer treatment from 2020-2025. Covers inhalable systems for pulmonary deposition, stimuli-responsive nanocarriers, and nano-immunotherapies. Highlights the evolution of nanocarriers from passive drug delivery vehicles to active participants in treating and tracking disease, with multiple platforms achieving clinical evaluation or regulatory approval.

## Nanocarrier Platforms

| Platform | Key Advantage | Lung Cancer Application |
|----------|--------------|------------------------|
| **Liposomes** | Biocompatible, clinical track record | pH-sensitive drug release, immunoliposomes |
| **Polymeric NPs** (PLGA) | Biodegradable, tunable release | Sustained-release chemotherapy |
| **Dendrimers** | Multivalent surface, precise architecture | Combination drug delivery |
| **Micelles** | Amphiphilic self-assembly | Hydrophobic drug solubilization |
| **Exosomes** | Natural cell-derived, low immunogenicity | Targeted cargo delivery |
| **Inorganic NPs** | Magnetic/optical properties | Iron oxide (imaging), hafnium oxide (radioenhancer) |
| **ADCs** | Antibody specificity + cytotoxic payload | HER2/TROP2-targeted conjugates |

## Innovative Delivery Strategies

### Inhalable Systems
- Direct pulmonary deposition bypasses systemic circulation
- Achieves high local drug concentration in lung tumors
- Reduces systemic toxicity (liver, kidney sparing)

### Stimuli-Responsive Release
- **pH-responsive**: Drug release triggered by acidic tumor microenvironment (pH 5.5-6.8)
- **Enzyme-responsive**: MMP-cleavable linkers
- **Hypoxia-responsive**: Nitroimidazole-based triggers in hypoxic tumor cores

### Nano-Immunotherapy
- Immune checkpoint modulators delivered via nanocarriers
- Nanoparticle vaccines encoding tumor antigens
- Combination of nano-delivery with anti-PD-1/PD-L1

## Clinical Progress (2020-2025)

| Agent | Target | Phase | Key Result |
|-------|--------|-------|------------|
| **T-DXd** (trastuzumab deruxtecan) | HER2-mutant NSCLC | Approved | ~55% response rate |
| **Datopotamab deruxtecan** | TROP2 | III | Met PFS endpoint |
| **BNT116** | Multivalent mRNA vaccine | I/II | Antigen-specific immune responses |
| **NBTXR3** | Hafnium oxide radioenhancer | I | Established feasibility |

## Significance

- Maps the full nanocarrier landscape for lung cancer from bench to clinic
- Highlights inhalable delivery as a lung cancer-specific advantage not available for other cancers
- Documents the clinical maturation of nano-ADCs (T-DXd, datopotamab deruxtecan)
- Positions nanocarriers as evolving from passive vehicles to active theranostic agents

## Limitations

- Broad scope — peptide functionalization is one component among many
- Many platforms still in early pre-clinical stages
- Translation challenges: scale-up, batch-to-batch reproducibility, regulatory pathways
- Inhalable systems face particle engineering challenges
