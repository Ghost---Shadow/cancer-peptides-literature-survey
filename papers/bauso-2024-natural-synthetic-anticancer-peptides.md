## Summary

A comprehensive review cataloging natural and synthetic anticancer peptides across 10 cancer types (lung, breast, colorectal, prostate, gastric, hepatocellular, melanoma, brain, osteosarcoma). Covers peptide sequences, sources, mechanisms of action, and in vivo efficacy data. Notes over 1,000 clinical trials using ACPs, positioning peptide-based nanosystems as a future therapeutic paradigm.

## Mechanisms of Action (General)

1. **Membrane disruption** — amphipathic peptides exploit anionic PS on cancer cell surfaces
2. **Apoptosis induction** — caspase activation, Bax/Bcl-2 modulation
3. **DNA damage** — direct nuclear interactions
4. **Angiogenesis inhibition** — VEGF/VEGFR pathway suppression
5. **Immunomodulation** — T cell activation, immune checkpoint modulation
6. **Pathway modulation** — NF-κB, AKT, ERK, SMAD4 signaling disruption

## Challenges for Clinical Translation

- Low half-life and bioavailability
- Limited intestinal permeability (oral delivery)
- Susceptibility to enzymatic degradation
- Molecular weight constraints

## Key Nanosystem Solutions

- Nanoparticle conjugation improves absorption and distribution
- Bypasses enzymatic degradation
- Enables targeted delivery to tumor sites
- Reduces off-target toxicity

## Significance

- Most comprehensive cancer-type-organized catalog of ACPs with sequences
- Covers 10 tumor types with specific peptide-target-mechanism mappings
- Provides AC-P19M sequence (FAKKLAKLKKKLAKLAKKR) not found in the original Shin et al. paper
- Notes >1,000 clinical trials using ACPs — demonstrating real clinical momentum

## Limitations

- Review article — no new experimental data
- IC50 values not systematically reported
- Many peptides still in early pre-clinical stages
- Clinical trial success rates not analyzed
