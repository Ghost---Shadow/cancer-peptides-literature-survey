## Summary

This review describes the concept of "molecular chimeras" — stable plant-derived peptide scaffolds (cyclotides and sunflower trypsin inhibitors/SFTI) grafted with cancer-targeting epitopes to create hybrid therapeutic peptides. The authors compare these grafted peptides against antibodies, small molecules, and peptide-drug conjugates, arguing that molecular chimeras combine the stability of natural scaffolds with the specificity of designed epitopes. Key targets discussed include HER2, EGFR, PD-1/PD-L1, VEGF, and integrins, with particular relevance to lung, breast, and colon cancers.

## Background & Motivation

- Current cancer therapies (chemotherapy, targeted small molecules, antibodies) face limitations: toxicity, drug resistance, immunogenicity, high production costs
- Antibodies, while effective (e.g., trastuzumab, pembrolizumab), can generate immune responses upon repeated administration and are expensive to produce
- Peptides offer advantages: smaller size, lower immunogenicity, easier synthesis, better tissue penetration
- However, linear peptides suffer from proteolytic degradation and short half-life
- Plant-derived peptide scaffolds (cyclotides, SFTI-1) possess exceptional stability due to cyclic backbone and disulfide bonds — potential ideal frameworks for grafting cancer-targeting sequences

## Key Topics Covered

### Cancer Therapeutic Landscape

- **Chemotherapy**: Alkylating agents, antimetabolites, plant alkaloids (vincristine, paclitaxel), topoisomerase inhibitors — limited by toxicity and resistance
- **Small molecule inhibitors**: Tyrosine kinase inhibitors (erlotinib, gefitinib for EGFR-mutant NSCLC), ALK inhibitors (crizotinib), BRAF inhibitors — resistance emerges
- **Monoclonal antibodies**: Trastuzumab (HER2), cetuximab (EGFR), pembrolizumab (PD-1), bevacizumab (VEGF) — immunogenicity and cost limitations
- **ADCs**: Antibody-drug conjugates (T-DXd, enfortumab vedotin) — combine targeting with cytotoxic payloads
- **Peptide-drug conjugates**: Emerging alternative to ADCs with smaller size and lower immunogenicity

### Peptide Scaffold Platforms

| Scaffold | Source | Structure | Key Stability Features |
|----------|--------|-----------|----------------------|
| **Cyclotides** | Plants (Violaceae, Rubiaceae) | ~28-37 aa, head-to-tail cyclic | Cyclic cystine knot (CCK) motif, 3 disulfide bonds, resistance to proteolysis, thermal stability |
| **SFTI-1** | Sunflower seeds (*Helianthus annuus*) | 14 aa, cyclic | Single disulfide bond, smallest known cyclic inhibitor, Bowman-Birk fold |
| **Kalata B1** | *Oldenlandia affinis* | 29 aa, prototypic cyclotide | CCK motif, oral stability, membrane interaction capability |

### Grafting Strategy — Molecular Chimeras

- **Concept**: Replace a loop region of the stable scaffold with a bioactive epitope sequence targeting a cancer protein
- **Cyclotide grafting**: Loops 1, 2, 5, or 6 of cyclotides (e.g., MCoTI-II, kalata B1) replaced with targeting sequences
- **SFTI grafting**: Active loop of SFTI-1 replaced with cancer-targeting epitope
- **Advantages**: Grafted peptide retains scaffold stability while gaining new biological function (cancer targeting)

### Cancer Targets and Grafted Peptide Examples

| Target | Disease Relevance | Grafting Examples |
|--------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **HER2** | Breast cancer, NSCLC | Peptides targeting HER2 dimerization interface grafted into SFTI-1 and cyclotide scaffolds |
| **EGFR** | NSCLC, colorectal cancer | EGFR-binding epitopes grafted into SFTI-1 (e.g., SFTI-G5 from Singh et al.) |
| **PD-1/PD-L1** | Multiple cancers, immunotherapy | Checkpoint-blocking peptide sequences grafted into cyclotide frameworks |
| **VEGF/VEGFR** | Anti-angiogenesis | Anti-VEGF epitopes in cyclotide loops |
| **Integrins (RGD)** | Tumor vasculature targeting | RGD-containing loops in cyclotides for integrin-mediated targeting |
| **p53-HDM2** | Tumor suppressor reactivation | p53-derived helical peptides grafted into cyclotide scaffolds |

### Design and Computational Methods

- **Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations**: Assess stability of grafted constructs, predict structural integrity after epitope insertion
- **Molecular docking**: Evaluate binding affinity of chimeric peptides to target proteins
- **Structure-activity relationship (SAR)**: Optimize grafted sequence length, position, and flanking residues
- **Solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS)**: Chemical synthesis of grafted peptides; native chemical ligation for cyclization

## Significance

- Provides conceptual framework for "molecular chimera" approach to peptide-based cancer therapeutics
- Directly relevant to lung cancer: EGFR and HER2 dimerization-targeting grafted peptides (e.g., SFTI-G5) have shown efficacy against NSCLC
- Bridges the gap between unstable linear peptides and expensive antibodies
- Cyclotide and SFTI scaffolds offer oral bioavailability — a major advantage over most peptide therapeutics
- Connects to Singh et al. (2025) paper on SFTI-G5 oral EGFR-targeting peptide already covered in this survey (Paper 13)

## Limitations

- Review is primarily conceptual; most molecular chimera examples are at pre-clinical or early research stages
- Limited quantitative comparison of grafted peptides vs. antibodies in terms of efficacy, pharmacokinetics, and cost
- Cyclotide grafting can disrupt scaffold stability if loop replacement is too large or structurally incompatible
- Manufacturing scalability of cyclotides (requiring native chemical ligation for cyclization) remains challenging vs. linear peptides
- Immunogenicity of plant-derived scaffolds in humans not fully characterized
- No clinical trial data for any molecular chimera peptide discussed
- Limited discussion of specific lung cancer applications beyond EGFR/HER2 targeting
