## Summary

This study designed NKTP-3, a cyclic D-peptide that simultaneously targets NRP1 (neuropilin-1) on the cell surface and intracellular KRASG12D — a previously "undruggable" oncogenic driver mutated in many lung cancers. NKTP-3 demonstrated strong biostability, cell permeability, and potent antitumor activity in both xenograft and KRASG12D-driven primary lung cancer models without obvious toxicity.

## Background & Motivation

- KRASG12D is one of the most common KRAS mutations in lung cancer, historically considered undruggable
- NRP1 is overexpressed on many tumor cells and promotes cancer progression
- Dual-targeting (surface receptor + intracellular oncogene) can improve specificity and efficacy
- D-amino acid peptides resist proteolytic degradation, solving the biostability problem of L-peptides
- Cyclic structure enhances cell permeability and target binding

## Methods

- **Virtual screening**: Structure-based computational design to identify NRP1/KRASG12D dual-binding peptides
- **Peptide synthesis**: Four candidate cyclic D-peptides (NKTP-1 through NKTP-4) synthesized and evaluated
- **Binding assays**: Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) and/or binding affinity measurements for both targets
- **Cell-based assays**: Proliferation inhibition in A427 (KRASG12D) lung cancer cells
- **Signaling analysis**: Western blot for downstream pathway inhibition (AKT, ERK phosphorylation)
- **In vivo models**: A427 xenograft and KRASG12D-driven primary lung cancer models

## NKTP-3 — Lead Compound

### Dual-Targeting Mechanism
1. **Step 1 — Surface binding**: NKTP-3 binds NRP1 on the cancer cell membrane
2. **Step 2 — Cell entry**: NRP1-mediated internalization delivers the peptide intracellularly
3. **Step 3 — Intracellular target engagement**: NKTP-3 binds KRASG12D inside the cell
4. **Step 4 — Signaling blockade**: Inhibits downstream AKT and ERK phosphorylation, suppressing proliferation

### Selectivity Profile

| Target | NKTP-3 Binding |
|--------|---------------|
| NRP1 | Strong binding |
| KRASG12D | Strong binding |
| NRP2 | No binding |
| KRAS wild-type | No binding |
| KRASG12C | No binding |
| KRASG12V | No binding |

This exquisite selectivity for KRASG12D over other KRAS variants and for NRP1 over NRP2 is a key advantage.

### Key Properties
- **Biostability**: D-amino acids confer resistance to protease degradation
- **Cell permeability**: Cyclic structure enables efficient cellular uptake
- **Dual-targeting**: Simultaneous engagement of surface (NRP1) and intracellular (KRASG12D) targets
- **Selectivity**: No activity against KRAS WT, G12C, or G12V variants

## Key Results

### In Vitro
- Significantly inhibited growth of A427 cells (human lung cancer with KRASG12D mutation)
- NKTP-3 was the most potent among four candidates (NKTP-1 to NKTP-4)
- Inhibited AKT and ERK phosphorylation downstream of KRAS

### In Vivo
- **Xenograft model**: Strong antitumor activity against A427-derived tumors
- **Primary lung cancer model**: Effective in KRASG12D-driven primary lung cancer
- **Safety**: No obvious toxicity observed in animal studies

## Significance

- First cyclic D-peptide demonstrated to dually target a cell-surface receptor (NRP1) and an intracellular oncoprotein (KRASG12D)
- Addresses the "undruggable" KRASG12D target through an innovative peptide-based approach
- D-peptide design solves the major biostability limitation of conventional peptide therapeutics
- NRP1-mediated internalization provides an elegant cell-entry mechanism without requiring separate cell-penetrating peptide motifs
- Provides a potential chemotherapeutic agent for KRASG12D-driven lung cancer

## Limitations

- Full peptide sequence not available from accessible sources
- In vivo pharmacokinetics (half-life, biodistribution) details not extracted
- Clinical translation pathway (formulation, dosing) not discussed
- Activity against other KRASG12D-mutant cancer types not tested
